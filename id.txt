Okay. This is one of those moments where the client said a word, nodded confidently, and walked away, leaving you to translate vibes into UX. Classic.

Let’s decode **“Campaigns” in a Web3 context** first, then I’ll give you a **clear layout idea** you can actually build.

---

## What “Campaigns” probably means here (Web3 translation)

Not political. Not marketing fluff. In Web3, **Campaigns usually mean:**

* Bounty programs
* Hiring challenges
* Contributor drives
* Short-term paid tasks
* Ambassador programs
* Grant-style applications

So instead of:

> “We are hiring a frontend dev”

It becomes:

> “Build X feature, promote Y product, test Z protocol, submit proof, get paid or get hired.”

Think:

* Jobs = long-term roles
* Campaigns = short-term, task-based opportunities with rewards

That fits Web3 culture perfectly.

---

## What EarnFI should feel like

**EarnFI = “make money by contributing”**

Tone:

* Clean
* Opportunity-focused
* Slightly gamified
* Trust-heavy (Web3 users are allergic to scams)

No noise. No fluff. Just opportunities.

---

## Suggested EarnFI Structure

### 1. EarnFI Landing Page

Top section:

* Big headline:
  **“Earn in Web3 by Building, Contributing, and Competing”**
* Subtext:
  *Jobs, bounties, and campaigns from Web3 founders and investors.*

Two main tabs or filters:

* **Jobs**
* **Campaigns**

---

## 2. Jobs Section (Straightforward)

This is your classic job board, but Web3 flavored.

Each Job Card:

* Project / Company logo
* Job title
* Short description
* Tags: `Remote`, `DAO`, `Paid`, `Equity`, `Token`
* Apply button

Job Details Page:

* Job image/banner
* Description
* Responsibilities
* Requirements
* Rewards (salary, tokens, equity)
* **Apply by submitting link**

  * CV
  * Portfolio
  * GitHub
  * Notion
  * Google Doc

No resume uploads. Web3 people hate that.

---

## 3. Campaigns Section (This is where it gets interesting)

### What a Campaign looks like

A campaign is a **goal-driven opportunity**, not a role.

Examples:

* “Build a demo for our protocol”
* “Create content explaining our product”
* “Find security bugs”
* “Onboard 100 users”
* “Community ambassador program”

Each Campaign Card:

* Campaign image
* Title
* Short goal statement
* Reward type: `USDC`, `ETH`, `Tokens`, `Job Offer`
* Timeframe or deadline
* Join / Participate button

---

### Campaign Details Page

This page should feel almost like a quest.

Sections:

* Campaign overview
* Tasks / Requirements
* Reward structure
* Submission rules
* Deadline
* Submit proof link (GitHub, Drive, Notion, Loom, etc.)

Bonus Web3 touch:

* “Top contributors may be offered a full-time role”

That’s how you bridge campaigns → jobs.

---

## 4. Submissions (Keep it stupid simple)

Users submit:

* A URL
* Optional note

That’s it.

No uploads.
No forms with 15 fields.
No suffering.

---

## 5. Investor / Founder Side (Posting)

Posting flow should be dead simple:

Choose:

* Job or Campaign

Then:

* Image upload
* Title
* Description
* Reward / Pay
* Link (external or internal)
* Publish

Web3 founders do not like admin dashboards. They like speed.

---

## Visual Style Ideas

Since this is Web3:

* Dark mode first
* Minimal cards
* Soft glow accents
* Clear reward labels
* No corporate HR energy

Think:

> “Opportunities board”
> not
> “Careers page from 2009”
