Cool, VPS + Hestia. Powerful combo. Also a fantastic way to accidentally look like a spam lord if it’s not tuned. Gmail saw your server and went “absolutely not”.

Here’s the no-nonsense checklist for Hestia on a VPS. Do these and Gmail usually calms down.
1. SPF (mandatory, not optional)

If SPF is wrong or missing, Gmail already distrusts you.

Where in Hestia

    DNS → your domain → Edit → Add record

SPF record

Type: TXT
Name: @
Value: v=spf1 ip4:YOUR_SERVER_IP -all

Replace YOUR_SERVER_IP with your VPS IP.

If you send mail using anything else later (SendGrid, Gmail SMTP), you’ll add it here.
2. DKIM (this one is huge)

Without DKIM, Gmail treats your emails like anonymous notes slipped under a door.

In Hestia

    Mail → Domains → select your domain

    Enable DKIM Support

    Save

Then:

    DNS → Add the DKIM TXT record Hestia shows you

It’ll look like:

Type: TXT
Name: mail._domainkey
Value: v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBgkq...

Do not edit the key. Do not get creative.
3. DMARC (Gmail loves this)

This tells Gmail “yes, I am serious about not being spam”.

DNS record

Type: TXT
Name: _dmarc
Value: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:postmaster@yourdomain.com

Later you can change p=none to quarantine or reject when things are stable.
4. Reverse DNS (PTR record)

This one blocks a shocking number of VPS users.

Your IP must resolve back to your domain.

Example:

102.68.84.118 → mail.yourdomain.com

You do this in your VPS provider’s panel, not Hestia.

Then make sure:

mail.yourdomain.com → 102.68.84.118

Forward + reverse must match. Gmail checks this. Religiously.
5. Hostname must match

On the server itself:

hostnamectl

Should show:

mail.yourdomain.com

If not:

hostnamectl set-hostname mail.yourdomain.com

Reboot after. Yes, actually reboot.
6. Stop Exim from blasting emails

Exim on Hestia will happily send 500 emails in 10 seconds and ruin your reputation.

Limit sending rate
Edit:

/etc/exim4/exim4.conf.template

Add or adjust:

smtp_accept_max = 20
smtp_accept_queue = 10

Restart Exim:

systemctl restart exim4

7. Check your IP reputation (brace yourself)

Many VPS IPs are born guilty.

Check:

    Spamhaus

    Barracuda

    SORBS

If listed, Gmail will keep blocking you no matter how innocent you act.

If it’s dirty:

    Request delisting or

    Ask your VPS provider for a new IP

8. Warm up your IP (do NOT spam test emails)

First few days:

    Send 5–10 emails max per day

    Only to people who reply

    No bulk, no newsletters, no “testing testing testing”

Gmail watches behavior like it’s stalking you.
9. If this still fails (real talk)

Running your own mail server in 2026 is basically choosing pain.

The sane move:

    Keep Hestia for inboxes

    Use SendGrid / Mailgun / Amazon SES for outgoing mail

    Add their SMTP to Hestia

That’s how most “serious” setups survive Gmail.

Your VPS isn’t broken. It’s untrusted.
Fix SPF, DKIM, DMARC, PTR, hostname, slow Exim down, warm the IP.

Do those and Gmail stops treating your server like it’s selling fake crypto in DMs.
